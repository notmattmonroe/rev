<a href="http://theskepticsguide.org/">The Skeptics Guide to the Universe</a> has <a href="http://www.kgoam810.com/showdj.asp?DJID=3542">Dr Dean Edell</a> this week.  This was a real blast from the past.  I grew up in the Bay Area and listened to his show and read his articles all through high school and college.  He was a tremendous influence on my skepticism in particular as they regard health claims.  It was nice to hear that he's still out there and fighting the good fight.<br /><br />In interesting juxtaposition my mother in law just gave my lovely wife a weight loss book by convicted fraudster Kevin Trudeau.  He has been barred "from appearing in, producing, or disseminating future infomercials that advertise any type of product, service, or program to the public" so egregious are his various frauds.  In the past he has posed as a doctor, forged checks, made false credit card charges, and then moved into nutritional supplements where ther real money is.  He's been shut down several times by the SEC, the FTC, various states.  The book in question is the one that finally got the courts to bar him from every benig involved in an infomercial or in selling or promoting any products or services.  <br /><br />The book claims to be an easy to do weight loss plan that anyone can do at home.   However, it actually is a very intense and expensive unproven scheme involving intense dieting, lifelong strict diet, and daily injections of human chorionic gonadotropin, an expensive prescription drug unproven for weight loss.  From the FTC:  <blockquote>To obtain the drug, a consumer would need to either go overseas, or find a doctor in the U.S. who will prescribe the drug for off-label use. The injections must be intramuscular, and Trudeau even instructs the dieter to do the injections under the care of a licensed physician. Besides the injections, this phase also requires a 500 calorie/day diet for 21 to 45 days, and the consumer cannot use any medicines, including over-the-counter and prescription drugs, most cosmetics, and no creams, lotions, or moisturizers.</blockquote><br /><br />I warned my wife against the book, of course.  Her mother unfortunately is a lost cause.  The family doctor she's been seeing regularly for many years she doesn't trust.  Any new age shill or nutritional supplement huckster who comes along she will believe every single word they say without question.
