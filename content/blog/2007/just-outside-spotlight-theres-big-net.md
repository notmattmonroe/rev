Things I learned as a theatre tech that apply to being a parent. Particularly a parent of a sleeping infant. <br /><ol><li>Walk quietly, noise from backstage will break suspension of disbelief </li><li>Talk quietly, see above </li><li>Light is bad </li><li>Dark clothes help you stay out of sight </li><li>Look carefully before picking something up or trying to move anything, will this make noise? </li><li>If you have an opportunity to eat, do so. The chance may not come again for a long time </li><li>Same with using the bathroom </li><li>Caffeine is your friend </li><li>Jeans are quieter than Dockers. </li><li>Soft soled shoes are better, if they are sturdy enough to be safe </li><li>Sometimes you have to crawl to avoid being seen </li><li>mini mag lites are discreet enough if you absolutely must have light </li><li>Know where the set pieces are, so you don't bump into them in the dark, thus making noise and/or injuring yourself </li><li>Props are to be put away when not in use. This avoids them being broken and people being injured by tripping over them. Usually in the dark. </li><li>Costume changes are frequent and inevitable. Get good at doing them fast. </li><li>The drama queen will almost always ultimately get her way. Strive to minimize the perceived value of throwing tantrums. </li><li>You are not the reason for the show. You are there to facilitate the show. People will pay to see performers on a bare stage with house lights on. They will not pay to see scene changes and light cues being run. Techies shouldn't have egos. </li><li>Be aware of the environment, watch for potential hazards that the talent may not notice. </li><li>Your workday starts before the talent wakes up, and ends long after they have finished their day. </li><li>If anything is making noise that shouldn't, apply duct tape as necessary.  This includes cats. </li></ol>
