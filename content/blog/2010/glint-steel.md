Title: A glint of steel
Date: 2010-05-17 17:11
Slug: glint-steel
Category: personal
Status: published

Today in workshop stories we tell a tale of how to get hurt in the shop.  I was working on the jewelry case.  I finished the carcass and based on the measurements decided to rethink everything else.  Interior measurements are 22 1/8 by 31 3/4.  I had planned on it being two sections which would mean drawers 15 1/2 inches wide.  That seems a little cumbersome and inelegant.  The clamping here is probably the most clamps I've used on a simple box ever, but this thing has GOT to be tight.  It's off by about 1/32" at the top right, which I'm willing to accept.<br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_carcass.jpg" alt="basic carcass" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_clamps.jpg" alt="clamping" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br />So I sat down and mapped out how I want to change everything, it now will be 3 sections, roughly 10" wide.  The left compartment will have 10 2" tall drawers for rings et al.  The center section will have two 7" drawers for large bracelets, brooches, etc and an open section for a yet to be design earring storage system.  The right compartment will have four 4" drawers and a 6" drawer all for bracelets and hair pins and the like.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_notebook2.jpg" alt="design" width="300" height="400" /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_notebook1.jpg" alt="step checklist" width="300" height="400" /><br /><br />I cut the two dividers and glued them up nicely and I'm happy that they're straight and true and square and all that jazz both side to side and front to back.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_divider.jpg" alt="left divider" width="300" height="400" /><br /><br />Then I started cutting the drawer parts.  I had an 8' 1x8 so I ripped it to 4 1 5/8" strips (the rails will be 3/8, so that makes 2" of height for each drawer section, hmmm, had I been thinking more clearly I'd have cut to a full 2 and overlapped the rails for a cleaner look.  I'll have to figure that out later, the hazards of redesigning on the fly).  Then I went to cross cut the strips.  I don't have a compound mitre saw which would be the preferred way to do this.  I suppose I could have hand cut it and in retrospect that would have been the best way to do it for several reasons.  Cutting 20 10" pieces and 20 6 1/2" pieces is tedious.  <br /><br />I set up the table saw to use the fence as a guide.  Yes, I know.  Major no-no.  I should have used a stop block instead. I have neither blade guard or riving knife on the saw.  OK, EVERYONE takes off the blade guard, and the one on my saw was actually a hazard as it was obscuring my ability to see anything, the so called riving knife was as bad, often preventing stock from being pushed through rather than preventing kickback.  I was using a push stick and wearing eye and ear protection and work gloves, so I wasn't entirely lackadaisical.  It went smoothly until I was on the last half dozen 6 1/2" pieces, and one bound between the fence and the back of the blade, inevitably shooting back at me.  The wood split, though I'm not sure if that happened on the saw or when it hit me.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_projectile.jpg" alt="The wood that hit me" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br />It hurt quite a bit, but it easily could have been far far worse.  If I had a more powerful table saw it would have been very bad indeed.  So here's to cheap underpowered tools.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/case/jcase_ouch.jpg" alt="the mark" width="400" height="300" /><br />This was taken a few minutes after (weird angle, this is pointing down at my chest), before it started bleeding.  Definitely still hurts, very tender right now.  I'm sure I've got a bruised rib, but I don't think it's anything more serious than that.
