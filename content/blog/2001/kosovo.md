I was opposed to the bombing in principle, I still think it was a mistake, but the pure and unabashed hypocrisy of bashing Clinton because US troops may get killed, and then turning around and bashing him after he's succeeded in his stated goals because NO US troops were killed is simply disgusting. And yet we are constantly reminded of the pervasiveness of the &quot;liberal media&quot;. It is too laugh.<BR><BR>Once again the world is faced with &quot;ethnic cleansing&quot;. Assuming this is true, what is the answer? As a committed leftist, I tend to view violence as an abhorrent solution. When violence is needed, it is usually as a result of poor foresight in dealing with earlier problems. One need only look at World War II for the prime example of this. The Versailles Treaty was a vicious and poorly conceived treaty, that can be clearly seen as a primary cause of every conflict in Europe since. Had the Allied powers been more willing to agree to a reasonable compromise rather than focusing entirely on revenge and punishment, the situation in Germany would not have been ripe for the rise of someone like Hitler.<BR><BR>The forced separation of many different peoples from their traditional homeland (Germans, Czechs. Serbs, etc etc) could only lead to conflict. We've seen it in Croatia, we're seeing it in Kosovo, we saw it fifty years ago in Germany. We will see it again. Super powers have had a tendency through history to carve up land based upon their desire for profit and easy exploitation with no regard for the people who've lived there for centuries. In their truly infantile myopia they refuse to consider that there may be repercussions when the enslaved (for there can be no other word for it) peoples wish to reunite with their brothers, sisters, cousins, and culture that they have been forced apart from. It is happening throughout Africa, it has happened and will happen again in Asia. The Spaniards seem to have had more foresight in this area than most, by simply wiping out the civilized people they encountered, and enslaving the less advanced and nomadic cultures that surrounded. This ensured that there would be no problem with the oppressed trying to reclaim their birthright.<BR><BR>However, is ethnic cleansing really happening? According to NATO the Serb military and freelance &quot;enforcers&quot; have killed about 2000 people since summer of 1998, the time at which the Serbs began retaliating against civilians for the guerilla attacks (by the foreign-funded KLA) on police stations and civilians. As Noam Chomsky noted, how do you think the US would react to attacks on police stations and civilians in New York by a guerilla army funded by Libya? Don't we support the same sort of actions in Turkey against the Kurds that we are opposing in Kosovo? Any leader who engages in activities that the US doesn't approve of is instantly compared to Hitler and accused of genocide.<BR><BR>Saddam Hussein, an evil man if ever there was one, was encouraged and supported by the US throughout the 80s', when he was an obedient dictator who did what we told him. Once he decided to start taking some of the profits from the oil fields back and use them to build up the infrastructure of his country he was labeled &quot;worse than Hitler&quot;. Mere days before he invaded, Ambassador April Glaspy indicated to him that the US would support him in reclaiming lands. However, once it became clear that he had no intention of passing over all the profits from those lands to the US, but rather wanted to build better roads and hospitals, it became necessary to demonize him and destroy him. Given the US record on involvement with smaller countries, I tend to disbelieve the official proclamations as to what is happening and why we are there.<BR><BR>I am reminded of a Clinton strategic document called Essentials of Post-Cold War Deterrence in Chomskys' article in Z magazine on Rogue States. This is of course reminiscent of Husseins' several offers to withdraw and surrender that were ignored because the US had to make an example of him (and did a notoriously bad job of it, at that).<BR><BR>All this being said, what is the answer? No nation is gong to voluntarily surrender a large chunk of real estate and many millions of citizens, as we can see quite clearly in the headlines. Look at Israel. I do not think that violence is the answer, I think that negotiated settlement is the path to resolution. What ultimately happens in these conflicts is that once thousands upon thousands have been killed, millions worth of property destroyed, then a negotiated settlement occurs. Why not skip the death and destruction phase of this process and go right to the agreement.<BR><BR>I think that the US bears a special responsibility for why that doesn't happen. People like Milosevic observe how the world reacts to these situations and decide that it is worth taking a chance that they will be allowed to simply slaughter the unwanted and take over their land. In most areas of Africa, the US has taken only marginal interest in protecting the rights of the oppressed, while in Europe the US has been more responsive. However, what Milosevic and his ilk are hoping is that it will be viewed as is East Timor.
