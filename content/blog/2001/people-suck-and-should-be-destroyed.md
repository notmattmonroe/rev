With all apologies to Henry Rollins.</h2><BR>Now, there are those who consider me to be a little reclusive, perhaps even anti-social. That's not true. I love to go out and hang out with my friends and so on and so forth. Problem being that I can't stand people. I like them fine individually, but en masse they just bug the hell out of me. Group dynamics tend to bring all members of the group down to the lowest common denominator, which frankly usually isn't all that brilliant. I don't have time for stupid people. I'm a busy guy, I've got things to do. Why would I want to kill several precious hours socializing with someone who is not only ignorant, but is PROUD of the fact that they're ignorant (that's a whole essay in and of itself!). The things I'm most irritated about right now, however, are three phenomenon that are far too common for my taste.<BR><BR>The first is smoking. I know all the addicts out there are going to scream &quot;I have the right to smoke! Fascist!&quot;. It's a truly odd world we live in when I am considered a fascist. You have the right to smoke until your right to smoke infringes on my right to not smoke. Just as your right to swing your arms around ends at my nose. Aside from the fact that smoking is about the most disgusting thing a person can do (I'd rather have the guy next to me on the street shooting heroin than smoking).<BR><BR>In the apartment I live in, we can't open our windows no matter how hot it is, because then our apartment fills with smoke. I can be anywhere, on the bus, at a restaurant, at work, anywhere, and I smell smoke. If you smoke you must be aware that it sticks to your clothes and hair and teeth and you smell gross. That's one of the things I really miss about California, is that you can smoke in your own house and that's pretty much it. No smoking in public, no smoking in the workplace, there are even many apartment buildings and condos that do not allow smoking at all. I'm strongly in favor of that. You think I should have to put up with your smoking? How would you feel if I wanted to smoke crack and blow all the smoke in your face?<BR><BR>Next up, we have cel phones. People who talk on cel phones while driving, specifically. I've nearly been killed more times than I care to count by some soccer mom or &quot;busy executive&quot; who is to busy talking to be bothered with little things like red lights or looking before merging. In San Francisco yesterday a woman in an SUV (naturally) rear-ended a police car at 35mph because she was too busy talking on her cel to notice those flashing red and blue lights (he was issuing a ticket at the time). She didn't even apologize, she was simply mad that they were wasting her time and made several calls while waiting for her ticket and then sped off to see if she could run over a kid on a bike next time.<BR><BR>I think there's a very simple solution to this problem. Two actually. Make it a crime to talk on the cel while driving is the most obvious, but it won't happen because no legislator has the guts to do it. The second, and I think more effective, would be financial. You cause an accident while on your cel? Fines are doubled and you lose your license for a month (the first time). Second offense? Fines are quadrupled and you lose your license for a year. Increment as you see fit. This fits in with my overall view on crime and punishment. For non-violent crimes jail is pointless.<BR><BR>You commit a crime? Pay cash. The greater the value of the crime, the more cash you have to pay. This would also solve the current inequities wherein a poor guy who robs a 7-11 of a couple hundred bucks gets 10 years in prison, while a financier who swindles his clients out of a couple million gets community service. Make them both pay cash. You steal $200, you owe $400. You steal $2,000,000, you owe $4,000,000. The rich are clamoring for the equality of a flat tax now, how about a flat fine structure?<BR><BR>Last, and probably least, comes the odd penchant people have for sharing the intimate details of their lives with complete strangers. This happens to me with disturbing frequency. People standing in line, people on the light rail, people in bookstores. Maybe it's just me they bug, maybe I have a sign saying &quot;I want to know all about your personal problems&quot;. I've had people reveal the details of their sex lives, their recent operation, theories about why it's everyone else's fault that they can't pay their rent. I've heard it all, and I really don't want to. I don't want to know that much about the people in the marketing department at work, let alone people I don't know at all. I suspect it's a safety issue, they'll never see me again so I can't judge them and they can get it off their chest and not feel embarrased. Got news for ya, I'm not a shrink and I'm not getting paid to listen to your problems.<BR><BR>
