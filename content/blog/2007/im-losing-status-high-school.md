Title: I'm losing status at the high school
Date: 2007-07-31 15:41
Slug: im-losing-status-high-school
Category: personal
Status: published

<br />I've been prompted by the <a href="http://stlbloggers.com/1684">blog carnival</a> to write something about high school, and I've also had this idea percolating in my mind for a while since Dirty Gale at work suggested it. <br /><br />I'm something of a music snob, I know you're shocked, and so people seem to think that I've always been 'cool' and 'hip'. Neither of which I am now nor have I ever been. In high school I was a stoner. I listened to some of the worst crap that came out in the 80's. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dio">Dio</a>. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helix_%28band%29">Helix</a>. Twisted Sister. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racer_X_%28band%29">Racer X</a>. <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accept_(band)">Accept</a>. Quiet Riot. Krokus. Autograph. And *shudder* <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryper">Stryper</a>. Sure, there were bands that I liked that were actually good. But I didn't really develop taste in music until I started working in a music store. <br /><br />In high school I was looking to define myself. I was a 'rebel'. Without, as it were, a cause. I latched on to metal because it was something that was bound to bother the whitebread neighborhood I grew up in. Except for all the other kids my age who were going through the same thing and listening to the same bands. And I embraced the culture wholeheartedly. <a href="http://www.imosh.com/WRISTBANDS.htm">Studded leather armbands</a>, lots of <a href="http://www.retailslut.com/">skulls and black clothes</a>, bandanas, torn jeans, denim jacket with all my favorite band logos all over it. When glam started making waves in the mid-80's with Ratt and Motley Crue I started wearing mascara and got my ear pierced and wore upside down cross earrings with snakes wrapped around them.  I was That Guy.<br /><br />I thought that being part of a group was good. It made me matter in some way. A part of something. An 'outsider' group was even better. I'd tried to fit in the other groups in high school and failed miserably. I was no good at sports. I wasn't geeky enough for <a href="http://www.chessclub.com/">Chess Squad</a> (though I was a member for a while). I got bored with <a href="http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/welcome">Dungeons and Dragons </a>pretty quickly. I had no discernable talent so I didn't really fit in with the theatre crowd, even though they were nice enough to let me hang out with them in spite of thinking I was a bit weird. Note that the theatre crowd thought I was weird. So I went down the social pecking order to the stoners. And I thought that here was a group that wouldn't have rigid standards of what was and was not 'cool' to do and would allow me to just be me without worrying about wearing the right clothes or having the right hair. I was of course completely wrong. As <a href="http://alternativetentacles.com/product.php?product=1305&sd=TgPPxmlpbwo6HwrZ2t0">Jello Biafra</a> famously noted &quot;No high school gym coach could get so many kids to dress exactly alike&quot;. <br /><br />I think that's when I started becoming cynical. <br /><br />It dawned on me in my Junior year that the entire social world of high school was ridiculous and that none if it actually mattered. None of us were cool. And all of us were cool, in our own way. Every group had the same basic dynamics regardless of if they were an 'in' group or an 'out' one. The chess geeks could be as vicious and stupid as the jocks. The stoners were just as superficial and self obsessed as the cheerleaders. So I stopped caring about being cool and being part of a group. I started listening to music that was 'not cool' by stoner standards. And I liked a lot of it. I started talking to whoever I wanted to regardless of what 'group' they were in. Some people were dicks about it, others were cool about it and we actually became friends. People still thought I was weird. But they also thought I was funny. It made the last two years of high school slightly less hellish than the previous two had been.
