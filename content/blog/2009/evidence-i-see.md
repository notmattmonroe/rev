It's Happy Darwin Day!  In honour of the progenitor of modern biology, take a few moments to peruse some of the commentary in the science blogosphere.<ul><li>John Whitfield finishes <a href="http://scienceblogs.com/bloggingtheorigin/2009/02/epilogue.php">Blogging the Origin</a> in his usual fine style.<li>John Wilkins posts another in his series of <a href="http://scienceblogs.com/evolvingthoughts/2009/02/myths_2_the_origin_of_species.php?utm_source=sbhomepage&utm_medium=link&utm_content=channellink">Debunking Darwin Myths</a><li>Alex Palazzo provides two key points on the evolution question, the most important of which is <a href="http://scienceblogs.com/transcript/2009/02/on_this_the_200_anniversary_of.php?utm_source=sbhomepage&utm_medium=link&utm_content=channellink">belief in evolution is irrelevant</a>.<li> D. Graham Burnett and Chris Mooney have a great history of the conflict between science and religion <a href="http://www.scienceprogress.org/2009/02/darwin-day-a-celebration-of-science-not-conflict/">as it relates to Darwin</a>.<li>Mooney also interviews <a href="http://www.scienceprogress.org/2009/02/darwins-dangerous-descendent/">Darwin's Dangerous Descendant</a>.  Which is a hilarious nickname.<li>Discovery has a <a href="http://dsc.discovery.com/earth/wide-angle/darwin-200-years.html">Darwin Portal</a> for learning more about the man and the theory.<li></ul>I think it's important to note, as many in the media often seem to miss and many in the ID movement intentionally misrepresent, that the Theory of Evolution has nothing to do with the question of the origin of life.  Even the title of the book, Origin of the *Species*, makes that clear.
