Title: I can rock the mic to silence by John Cage

There is a good reason we decided to make microphones for the girls rather than go out and buy them.  					A good reason.  A really good reason.  However, as I can't remember what exactly it was, I'll go with					it was cheaper. 					<p />				   <div class="contentblock1">				   <img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3771.JPG" width="300" height="225"/>					We start with a very simply styrofoam ball from the craft store, about four inches in diameter.					I was going to use a spade bit, but I only have a 1" bit and the dowel I bought was 1 1/4".  					So I dug through assorted drawers and bins and dug out a door lock drilling kit that had a 1 1/4" attachement.  					Sure, it didn't go deep enough, but I could resolve that problem fairly easily.										 					 					After drilling it down as far as possible, I used the spade bit in the handheld drill to clean out the hole.										</div>					<div class="contentblock2">					<img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3775.JPG" width="225" height="300"/>										Then I'd put the ball back in the drill press to get the hole a bit deeper, then clean out again with the 					handheld drill.  No, nothing is clamped in place.  Yes I am holding it still with my hand.					At least I'm wearing gloves. 					<br /><br />Sure, a chisel, file, or knife would have been safer and less prone to make a mess.  As <a href="http://www.redgreen.com/">Red Green</a> would say 					"It's not right, and it's not smart, but it's part of what makes us who we are."  Words to live by.					</div>				   <div class="contentblock1">					<img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3781.JPG" width="300" height="225" align="right"/><br /><br />					The next challenge was to round the bottom of the dowels.  I cut the dowel into six pieces roughly 7-8" long each.					My plan was to put the styrofoam ball on one end, and round the edges at the other end for 					both aesthetic and safety reasons.  How to round the edges cleanly on a circular piece of stock quickly.					The consensus view would be to use a lathe if you have one.  Not having a lathe, the next option would 					likely be to use (or build if you didn't already have) a v-jig that would hold it in place					safely while you turn it by hand.					<br /><br />										Alternately, you might consider throwing a <a href="http://www.rockler.com/articles/display_article.cfm?story_id=135&cookietest=1">roundover bit</a> 					on your router table and spin the dowel by hand with no guides.  Because that's a really good idea.					</div>					<div class="contentblock2">					<img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3817.JPG" width="300" height="225" />					Once I went through all that on all six microphone bodies it was on to the painting and decorating.    At the 					suggestion of coworker Bobbo I tried hanging them from a string stapled to the top (which would ultimately 					be covered by the styrofoam ball) and painting that way, but that technique really only works with spraying.  					If you're using a brush the workpiece just moves away from you.  So I ran screws through a piece of scrap 					and put the microphones on there for painting, which worked wonderfully.  Finishing is my least favorite part 					of woodworking.  I hate brushing as it aggravates my wrist, and I don't have room for a spray booth.  The only 					kind of finish work I enjoy is rub on stains because it's easy and doesn't hurt.  My standards are pretty reasonable, I think.					</div>				    <div class="contentblock1">				    <img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3827.JPG" width="300" height="225" />				    My lovely wife handled the decoration after I got the basic painting done, and did a wonderful job of it.  				    Then I glued on the tops and sprayed polyurethane over them to reduce the shedding of glitter.  As I was spraying the poly on I thought "Hmm, I wonder if poly and foam have any negative interactions.  Oh well, too late now." 					The flashy toy microphones run anywhere from ten dollars to thirty dollars (above that they start getting pretty fancy) and would 					likely on last a week or two in our house.  Our kids are tough on toys, particularly flimsy plastic.  So three microphones for 					our three girls would run at least thirty dollars and at least one would be trashed by the end of the month.  Instead we made 					six very unique and durable ones for a grand total of under twelve dollars.  Two are going to Boo's best friend and her best friend's little brother, 					one is going to another of her friends in California.  The other three will be lost under the bed by the time I'm done writing this.										</div>					<div class="contentblock2">				    Always be safe.				    <img border="0" src="/media/images/100_3778.JPG" width="400"height="300" />					</div>
