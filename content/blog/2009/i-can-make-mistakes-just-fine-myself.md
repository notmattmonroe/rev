Let's say you had marked all the posts on your wife's blog as 'unpublished'.  And let's say you really, really, REALLY, didn't want to walk through all those posts to find the mere 50 or so that she wanted to be republished.  I didn't find any obvious way to do it through the wordpress interface nor in any documentation with a cursory search.  <br /><br />As it turns out:  not that hard.  If you want to update all posts in a category in wordpress just follow these basic steps (assuming WP 2.3 or greater).<br /><br /><ul><li>Log in to your handy sql prompt and type the following line<br /><code>select term_id, name from wp_terms;</code><li>You'll want to note the term id (48 in my example) as you will use it when you type the following line<br /><code>select term_taxonomy_id from wp_term_taxonomy where term_id=48; </code><li>Use that term_taxonomy_id (52 in my example) in this handy little line<br />update wp_posts INNER JOIN wp_term_relationships on wp_posts.ID = wp_term_relationships.object_id set wp_posts.post_status='publish' where wp_term_relationships.term_taxonomy_id=52;</ul>Voila.  Now all the posts in category 48 are now published, and no others are.The deprecating of category in favor of terms seems to me needlessly complex, but PHP apps tend to go that way.<br /><br />Also, if you've never been able to fully wrap your head around joins, Atwood's <a href="http://www.codinghorror.com/blog/archives/000976.html">Visual Explanation of SQL Joins</a> is a must read.<br /><br />UPDATE: I've gotten a few emails on doing it all in one big multiple inner join statement.  Yes, if this were something I were going to do on a semi-regular basis I'd make the extra effort to turn it into a whole repeatable process.  This being a one off event, it didn't seem worth the extra effort.
