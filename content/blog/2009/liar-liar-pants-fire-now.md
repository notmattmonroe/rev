Inspired by <a href="http://www.xkcd.com">xkcd</a>, I've begun noting and cultivating some of my quirky observations into full on hobbies.  I've mentioned adding semi-plausible notes to whiteboards.<br /><br />   Tonight I was struck by an article in <a href="http://seedmagazine.com/">Seed</a>: the Prophetic Brain by <a href="http://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/Friston/">Karl Friston</a>.  The article largely revolves around the idea that part of the way our brain works to deal with reality is to limit the range of possible situations it has to be ready to cope with (e.g. you don't expend energy on being prepared to suddenly find yourself transported to the bottom of the sea).   I ran across the quote <blockquote> All quantities associated with an agent will change to minimize free-energy</blockquote>And I thought "Heh, it would be funny to start blogging on science topics and wildly misinterpret out of context quotes, like take this snippet and start ranting that big science and the oil/auto industry are  conspiring to keep energy expensive by covering up the existence of free energy."<br /><br />It was funny for about half a second until I realized that's exactly what the anti-science nutjobs and cranks do, only they are dead serious. Note that all free energy schemes have turned out to be run by either the willfully dishonest or genuinely inept.</ul>Not that I feel strongly on the topic or anything.
