A recurring topic in my social/web circles is diversity in the public faces of atheism, skepticism, and science.  Inevitably a loud contigent will try to shout down any discussion at all with "it doesn't matter what they look like, what matters is what they say!" In a perfect world that would be true.  In response I ask: If it doesn't matter then why do you get so upset when the possibility of having more non-white-non-straight-male presenters is brought up?  <p />The purpose of diversity in these contexts isn't for the benefit of the presenters, though they certainly benefit from getting more publicity and recognition that is a side effect not a primary intended effect.  The purpose is to benefit those in the audience.  It encourages other non-white-males to participate, to be heard, to be a force for bettering the world.  It also gives white males the chance to see things from a different perspective.<p />I am a middle aged middle class white male. If have the opportunity to hear two equally qualified speakers on a topic and one is a white guy and the other isn't, I will probably learn more from the one who isn't.  Even covering the exact same topic the non-white-male is going to have a different perspective.  I am overly familiar with the white guy perspective.  Everyone is, even if they aren't a white guy.  Listening to someone who comes to a skeptical topic, a political topic, a sociological topic from a completely different perspective allows me to benefit from their different experience and to learn far more than hearing a regurgitation of what is already pervasive in the discussions.
