Title: Stay hidden at the bottom
Date: 2010-05-25 17:16
Slug: stay-hidden-bottom
Category: personal
Status: published

As mentioned earlier, there are some risks associated with crosscutting on a table saw.  I have taken a safer approach more recently, and will refine more based on some excellent feedback on facebook.  I had to do more crosscutting because when I dry fit my test drawer I realized I had gotten the side pieces wrong, a full inch too long.  It's easier to remove wood than to add it back, so I ran the 20 sides through the trim process in no time.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/drawers/IMG_1003.JPG" alt="table saw crosscutting" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br />The interior measurements of the drawer are 5 1/2" by 9 7/16", so now I've got to get some foam and some jewelry friendly cloth.  I'll be relying on a quilting friend to help me get all that part right, I know nothing about fabric.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/drawers/IMG_1004.JPG" alt="measurements" width="414" height="300" /><br /><br />The dry fit is good.  I'll be putting up 3/8" rails and front crosspieces (for aesthetics).  10 of these drawers will make up the left hand compartment of the jewelry case.  Routing was a breeze, ran a 1/8"x1/8" dado about 1/8" from the bottom of the sides for the hardboard, and then did a 1/4" roundover on the inner and outer top.  There is sanding yet to do after the glue ups.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/drawers/IMG_1005.JPG" alt="drawer dry fit" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br />And the parts for the other 9 drawers stacked into the unit.  I'll start staining and doing the top/bottom trim on the carcass this week so Jaden can actually start using it (sans doors) as soon as June.<br /><br /><img src="/media/revmatt/drawers/IMG_1006.JPG" alt="parts is parts" width="400" height="300" /><br /><br />
