Title: Who Are The Brain Police
Date: 1997-12-20 15:46
Slug: who-are-brain-police
Category: personal
Status: published

On December 17, 1997, President Bill Clinton signed into law a bill which sets new definitions and penalties for Copyright protection on the internet. I will be among the first to concede that copyrights do need to be protected.<BR><BR>Writers, artists, musicians, and photographers get screwed enough by the companies that package, market and distribute their works that they don't need more screwing. I give credit to all the creators that I am inspired by or that I use examples from. I encourage you to patronize their works if you enjoy what they offer. This modification of existing copyright law is certainly justified, however the attached penalties are absurd in the extreme.<BR><BR>If you are found to violate less than $1000 worth of copyright (based on the current retail value) you can be fined $100,000 and spend a year in jail. This from the legislators who let the Savings and Loan criminals go free when they cost taxpayers and investors billions of dollars? Of course, people who violate copyrights on the internet do not give large contributions to political campaigns, while S&amp;L crooks do, so I certainly understand the difference in penalties. After all, when you violate a copyright, you cost one company and a few artists several dollars, which is much more significant than bilking investors and taxpayers out of billions of dollars.<BR><BR>By the logic of these penalties, if you were to cause an auto accident that resulted in $1,000 in property damage, you (or your insurance company, if you can still afford insurance) would have to pay the owner of the property $100,000. If this makes any sense to you, then I would like to talk to you about a business proposition... I suspect that these penalties are unconstitutional, and dearly hope that they will be found such by the Supreme Court in the near future.<BR><BR>I find it ironic that the &quot;small government, laissez-faire&quot; Republicans and conservative Democrats forget all about their &quot;ideals&quot; when it comes to the interests of big businesses who have paid a lot of good money to buy lots of politicians. If you want to know more about this issue, try reading all about H.R. 2655 at thomas.loc.gov.<BR>
