"You can't prove a negative."  The last refuge of the unsupported assertion.  Where does that idea come from?  Absurd.  In complicated cases it may well take a lot of work, but it's simple a falsehood that you cannot ever <a href="http://www.infidels.org/library/modern/richard_carrier/theory.html">prove a negative</a>.  Ask a mathemetician.  A more concrete example, I have a monitor on my desk in front of me.  I can absolutely and without a doubt prove that it is not a cat.  That is proving a negative.  What most people actually mean when they say you can't prove a negative is, I believe, that you can't prove non-existence.  Proving non-existence is a much much trickier proposition and if not impossible is close enough to it as to be indistinguishable.<br /><br />"Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."  True enough as far as it goes, which is not very.  I easily concede that it's impossible to entirely discount the temporal existence of <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicorn">unicorns</a>, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixie">pixies</a>, <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprite_(creature)">sprites</a>, or <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus">pegasus</a>.  This is a far cry from being able to seriously engage in a lengthy discussion of the attributes of said beings.  I will gladly posit the theory that http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lephrechaun">leprechauns</a> might be real rather than myth, but cannot accept any claims of their behaviors, rules regarding their gold, or even descriptions of what they look like without a great deal more evidence than currently has been presented (viz: none).<br /><br /><a href="http://www.randi.org/site/">James Randi</a> said recently that he is an atheist of the second definition.  In most dictionaries the first entry for atheist is 'one who believes there is no god', or a hard atheist in popular parlance.  The second definition, which is what every atheist I've ever known identifies with, is 'one who finds the evidence insufficient to believe in the existence of a god.'  This is a more nuanced position and in my opinion the only intellectually justifiable one.  One mustn't go far to expand this specific view on the question of the existence of a diety to any and all claims made, particularly of an exceptional nature.  Claims of UFO's, psychic abilities, the effectiveness of a wide range of methodologies for treating diseases, all come under the same set of rules:  I will not believe in it without sufficient evidence.  And the more implausible the claim based on current understanding of biology, chemistry, and physics the more high quality the evidence must be to make up for that deficit. "<a href="http://www.quackwatch.org/01QuackeryRelatedTopics/extraproof.html">Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.</a>" - <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan">Carl Sagan</a>. <br /><br />I'm often asked why I 'ignore all the evidence' where said evidence is low quality studies by dubious researchers with a stake in the game or even worse simply anecdotes.  I ignore that data because it's not worthwhile data.  My response is 'why do you accept such poor quality claims and call them evidence?'  A single pilot study with a half dozen participants is largely useless. A study that  hasn't been replicated by an independent lab is useless.  Studies published in low-tier journals are useless.  I won't waste my time even considering them if they can't pass the basics of scientific research.
