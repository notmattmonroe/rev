A bit of advice someone gave me that sounds very very good in theory but doesn't work out as expected:<p />Make it like it's for you.<p />The idea being that you will do better work if the end result is for you rather than for a customer or a friend or etc etc.  This is not always the case.  I embrace the 'good enough' mantra perhaps more than I should when I know that the end user is me.  Whether it's building my new desk, a bookshelf, or my website tools I'm content for it just to work.  Fit and finish are often left as 'maybe I'll get around to that someday'.  <p />Given how much I hate sanding and painting this bleeds into other projects as well, where the pieces are rougher than I'd like and the jointery isn't as tight as it should be.  The only thing I've built thus far that I'm 80% pleased with is the dresser for the master bedroom.  Everything else I want to throw out and start over.  <p />The current top project I'm slowing down, doing it as right as possible.  I spent 2 hours jointing and planing and ripping and replaning and sanding two six foot 1x6 boards.  I have high expectations of this.<p />
