Title: Jesus Thinks You're A Jerk
Date: 2001-10-12 14:43
Slug: jesus-thinks-youre-jerk
Category: personal
Status: published

The attempts to impose a Judeo-Christian morality upon this country have been occurring with distressing frequency of late. From banning adult-oriented material on military bases to banning abortion to banning books to banning access to information on safe sex and AIDS prevention, the religious right has been lobbying to have their own personal beliefs and convictions forced upon the rest of America against the will of the majority of the people.<BR><BR>The justification, aside from it being God's will their contention that America was founded on Christian ideals. They cite the Founding Fathers as shining examples of Christian piety. A cursory glance at the factual historical record reveals that our forefathers were no more noble or pious in their thoughts or actions than are the people of today. Many of the men who are considered to be the most influential and important in the creation and shaping of this nation were deists, agnostics, or atheists, and many of them openly ridiculed Christianity or at least actively distanced themselves from the church and its' preachings.<BR><BR>Washington himself once remarked that the United States was &quot;in no sense founded upon the Christian doctrine&quot;. Another respected philosopher and politician of the day, Thomas Paine, wrote that &quot;I do not believe in the creed professed by the Jewish church, by the Roman church, by the Greek church, by the Turkish church, by the Protestant church, nor by any church that I know of. My own mind is my own church.&quot; Thomas Jefferson was quoted as saying &quot;It does me no injury for my neighbor to say there are twenty gods or no god. It neither picks my pocket nor breaks me leg...I do not find in orthodox Christianity one redeeming feature.&quot;. The Republicans often turn to Lincoln as a staunch defender of the Christian way, yet Lincoln himself said &quot;The Bible is not my book, and Christianity is not my religion. I could never give assent to the long, complicated statements of Christian dogma.&quot;<BR><BR>Our founding fathers tended to be skeptical of religion and usually referred to Providence rather than to Christ or God. In 1776, the Continental Congress decreed a day of fasting and prayer to in which they specifically mentioned Jesus Christ, yet Washington intentionally omitted this reference when he repeated the admonition to his troops. Benjamin Franklin is thought to have believed in other gods, and believed strongly in an afterlife. Alexander Hamilton was even his day derided as being opportunistically religious, only using it to lambast his opponents and ignoring it otherwise. Rush Limbaugh has quoted James Madison as directing America to follow the Ten Commandments. No one has yet been able to find the source which Rush got the quote from, and the recognized expert on Madison, John Stagg of the University of Virginia, called the quote &quot;Inconsistent with Madison's other recorded opinions.&quot;***<BR><BR>With the primary excuse for such shameless demagoguery removed the truth is that there is no basis in rational thought or historical precedent for creating a theocracy in this country. I find it ironic that so many of these Christian Conservatives who demonize the religious theocracies in the Middle East are trying to create a Christian version of them here in America. They say they are the natural successors to the Holy Roman Empire, which was neither Roman nor an Empire, and it's Holiness was questionable at best. One man's morality is another man's belly-laugh, and there is no equitable means of determining a precise national moral code that can be said to be held by all or even a majority of the people.<BR><BR>Certain moral precepts can easily be held to be almost universal and there is no sane rational person who would oppose laws enforcing such morality. Laws such as those which are meant to prevent a person from killing another or taking another's property or livelihood are certainly among such laws. Laws on more personal issues, from drug use to prostitution to gambling to abortion, there is no true national consensus. If a majority of eligible, or even registered, voters were to participate in a national referendum on morality, there would be no majority who would agree on more than the most basic simple moral laws. Even Hammurabi's Code or the 10 Commandments contain elements which would cause dissent from many on philosophical or personal grounds. Any attempt to define a more precise and all encompassing moral code (read: Christian Theocracy) would be destined to fail as the morals of Christianity are not the morals of Islam are not the morals of Judaism are not the morals of Buddhists and so on.<BR><BR>Even in the realm of Christianity, there are dozens of stripes of Catholics and Christians who cannot agree with one another on a set of moral and theological precepts, how can anyone expect a nation comprised of several major and dozens of minor religions to be any more in agreement? To take a brief overview of legal aspects of religious legislation, the Supreme Court ruled in Abington School District v. Schempp (374 US 203 - 1963) that schools could not favor Christianity over other religions. Justice Tim Clark wrote, in the decision, &quot;In the relationship between man and religion, the state is firmly committed to a position of neutrality.&quot; Once again to Rush Limbaugh, one of the dominant voices in favor of a theocracy, he refers to a Supreme Court decision barring the 10 Commandments from classrooms, bemoaning the atheistic liberals on the bench. In reality, there was no such ruling. The Supreme Court recognizes the separation of Church and State.<BR><BR>History has shown time and again that attempts to legislate morality fail. They may fail quietly, through non-enforcement, or they may fail grandly, as prohibition and Joseph McCarthy did. Such attempts may temporarily succeed, but that merely makes their failure all the more damaging to the country as a whole. Robert Heinlein, in his Future History, predicts that a Christian Theocracy will come to power in America towards the end of the 20th century, plunging the country into first a military dictatorship, then civil war, and finally a holocaust of unimagined proportions. 1984 pales in comparison.<BR><BR>*Salvation for Sale by Gerard Thomas Straub.<BR>**May it Please the Court edited by Peter Irons and Stephanie Guitton.<BR>***The Way Things Aren't Steven Rendall, Jim Naureckas, and Jeff Cohen.
