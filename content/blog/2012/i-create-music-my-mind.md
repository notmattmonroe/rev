Title: I create music with my mind
Date: 2012-04-30 15:54
Slug: i-create-music-my-mind
Category: personal
Status: published

I am fascinated with the idea of object oriented pop.  An app that lets you drop in some chord progressions and churns out a fully formed pop song with all the appropriate characteristics is exciting and challenging.  I know there are apps that do this to a limited degree now.  A very very limited degree.<p />Purists of course bemoan the death of 'real music' with each technological advance.  The very idea of recorded music caused controversy at it's beginning and even today many musicians hew to the belief that the only 'real' musical experience is live music.  The systemization of music composition into a computer program is of course a logic extension of the history of technology changing music.  <p/>As voice synthesis progresses and apps get more nuanced and robust club music will be the innovator in this direction.  This is not a slam on club music, it's just the genre best suited to lead the way.  Is the rules-driven engine of method calls that produce predictable chains of results that wildly different than the workaday songwriters of yore that gave us the boy band bonanaza of the late 90's or the hair metal hegemony of the late 80's?   While the famed Brill building gave us many many classics of the era there was plenty of dreck created there as well.<p/>Frank Zappa was an early adopter of this concept, latching onto the Synclavier with the idea that he'd be able to faithfully reproduce his musical visions without having to rely on sketch and unreliable musicians (of whom he felt that the only ones more undisciplined and sloppy than rock and roll drummers were member of the London Symphony Orchestra).  I can only imagine what he'd have acheived had he lived another 30 years.  <p />The possibility of enabling more and more Rebecca Blacks* is more than balanced by the real possibilities here though.  A well designed musical rules engine with sufficient tweakability would be the greatest tools a composer could imagine.  Beat level fine tunability of every element of every sound at that point would be astounding.  Despite the 'human feel' options most rhythm machines now have the music would still I think be lacking that ineffable sense of humanity that so typifies great works, but as a tool for teaching music I think it would be incredible, and as a means of creating decent pop music as well.  For the true artists it would be an enabler of even greater works. And it's probably only 20-30 years away at this point...
